# M-19: Dan McDonald, Ownwell homeowner reports
source: Why_Every_Canadian_Broker_Needs_to_Know_About_Ownwell_with_D_mp3.txt
context: Manifest guess corrected to McDonald. Canadian, former head of product at Lendesk, not a broker. He founded and sells Ownwell, so this is vendor content and every result figure is his own marketing.

## Big idea
Send homeowners a monthly report on their own equity and buying power, because personal numbers create inbound conversations that generic newsletters and birthday cards never do.

## Journey practices
- [POST-FUND] Broker uploads the client database once. The platform auto enrolls each client in a personalised monthly report on home price, equity and mortgage.
- [POST-FUND] Before enrolling anyone the broker emails the database announcing the free tool, so the first report arrives anticipated rather than cold.
- [POST-FUND] Every regular newsletter repeats a reminder to watch for that month's report, per the vendor's onboarding guide.
- [RENEW] The equity figure homeowners have never seen is the stated top deal driver. Disbelief triggers the inbound call the broker then works.
- [REFER] The home price section invites the client to request a realtor partner consultation, so the estimate is engagement bait rather than a valuation.
- [REFER] Adopt My Mortgage gives annual plan brokers ten partner spots, each a unique link to a co-branded opt-in page.

## Gates
- No announcement email first, no strong open rate.
- No perfect data before enrolling. A wrong figure is a touch point the expert converts.
- No direct signup for partners. They reach the report only through a broker's link.
- No more than ten partner spots per broker.

## Numbers
Open rate about 65 percent, top performers above 75 or 80, report click rates about 55 percent [self-reported, and he sells the tool]. Ten partner spots per annual plan, capped at 250 brokers [stated]. One US user markets to 30,000 off 4,000 clients [stated].

## Delegation map
Broker uploads the list once and answers inbound replies. The platform enrolls, personalises and sends every monthly report and hosts the partner landing pages. A vendor designer owns the look. Principal minutes not stated.

## Solo translation
A solo agent replicates this directly, because the product is the automation and it replaces the assistant who would otherwise mail newsletters. What must stay human is the pre-announcement and the reply when a client disputes their equity figure, which is where the deal is made.

## Tensions
Against the common advice to market off rate moves and forecasts, he says timing a client's life is impossible, so send monthly and let them self-identify. He also argues against accuracy, since perceived home value drives the call, not an objective one. Nothing here is observed practice.
