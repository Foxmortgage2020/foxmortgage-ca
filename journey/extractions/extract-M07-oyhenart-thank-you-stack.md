# M-07: Chad Oyhenart, the two-step thank you
source: I_Love_Mortgage_Brokering_Ep_84__Chad_Oyhenart_mp3.txt
context: Richmond BC, owner of a DLC Aegis franchise with five other brokers. Twelve years in at recording. Episode 84, recorded 21 October 2015, by far the oldest file in the corpus.

## Big idea
Gratitude only produces referrals when it is systematised, because intentions to thank people reliably outrun the follow through.

## Journey practices
- [REFER] A referral arriving triggers a Redstamp card sent by text or email, chosen from a large template library and customised to name the person who referred.
- [REFER] That card pre-announces a second message, a Gusto gift card sent immediately after, so the thank you and the gift arrive as a pair.
- [REFER] For an older demographic he branches to a handwritten card with a gift card inside, then follows up by email or phone. He runs different processes by demographic deliberately.
- [REFER] Ran a giving program of 100 dollars per completed file to BC Children's Hospital, roughly 2011 to early 2014. It stopped when the foundation changed its process.
- [SIGN] MPP and life insurance are signed at the signing appointment rather than referred out first. His framing is that the client is covered now and can cancel after reviewing other options.
- [MODEL] Hires on family fit and full time only, and turns down brokers who do not fit.

## Solo translation
The Redstamp and Gusto pairing is a two-message sequence fired by one trigger, trivially automatable and needing no staff. The demographic branch is a template switch on a client field. Signing MPP at the appointment is a signing-package moment, not a campaign.

He crossed the cross-sell divide mid-career, moving from referring all insurance out to writing MPP himself. Age is the main caution, these practices predate the current renewal cycle.
