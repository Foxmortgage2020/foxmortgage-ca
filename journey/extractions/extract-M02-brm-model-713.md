# M-02: You don't need a bigger team, you need a better model (Ep 713)
source: 713-_You_Don_t_Need_a_Bigger_Team_You_Need_a_Better_Model9hfgr.txt
context: Peckford solo episode. Case study from a BRX mastermind team: 330+ files, $1.6M top-line revenue, three people total including both partner-brokers.

## Big idea
Experience quality per file beats headcount. One voice for the client, one voice for the referral partner, one shared process.

## Journey practices
- [MODEL] The BRM model: the salesperson does the strategy call, then one named person owns the file end to end, every document, every sign-off. Kills the underwriter-plus-fulfillment split that made clients ask who am I talking to. Modeled on Scotia and MapleTrust single-point underwriting.
- [REFER] The same BRM is permanently assigned to each key referral partner, so a partner only ever deals with the same two humans across every file. Partners later phoned to ask whether they still get their underwriter, the same loyalty that makes brokers follow a lender underwriter to a new bank.
- [MODEL] Two-partner structure: one partner runs people, the other runs process. Identical process is mandatory. Shared brand and infrastructure, independent books of business, cross-coverage when one is away.

## Gates
- One process. Per-partner process divergence is banned or the staff model collapses.

## Numbers
330+ files [stated]. $1.6M top-line [stated]. Three people including principals [stated].

## Delegation map
Principal: strategy call and the relationship. BRM: everything else on the file. Hiring rule: DISC high S and C only. Don't hire yourself, a high D and I hire is training your competition and won't stay.

## Solo translation
Mike already is the single point of contact, and this episode says that's an asset to preserve, not a limitation to escape. Automation has to keep one voice: agents draft, Mike sends. The DISC rule translates directly to agent design, build S-and-C automations that follow the playbook steadily, not D-and-I agents that improvise around it.

## Tensions
Direct argument against the celebrity model's hidden helpers and against multi-touch lender-style handoffs. He notes the benefit never shows in the P&L, it shows in referral volume, which is a warning about measuring this change by cost.
